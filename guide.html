Getting Started with Circuits

The foundation of all robotics. These 12 projects teach kids how electricity flows, what circuits are, and how to build simple but real electronic devices from scratch.

🔦
PROJECT 01
LED Flashlight Circuit
Ages 5+Easy⏱ 20 min
›
Materials
  • 1 LED (any color)
  • 1 AA battery
  • 2 wires (10 cm)
  • Electrical tape
  • Cardboard tube
Steps
  1. Strip wire ends with scissors
  2. Connect one wire to + battery terminal
  3. Connect other wire to – terminal
  4. Touch long LED leg to (+) wire
  5. Touch short LED leg to (–) wire
  6. LED lights up! Tape and insert into tube
What You Learn

Kids discover that electricity needs a closed loop to flow. Break the loop, light turns off — the "aha!" moment that starts every engineer's journey.

CircuitsPolarityConductors

PROJECT 07
Homemade Switch

›
Materials

  • 2 metal paper clips
  • AA battery + LED
  • 3 wires
  • Brass fasteners
Steps
  1. Push two brass fasteners through cardboard 3 cm apart
  2. Attach paper clips to fasteners — one pointing over the other
  3. Wire: battery (+) → first fastener → LED (+)
  4. Wire: LED (–) → second fastener → battery (–)

Motorized Racing Car
Ages 9+Medium⏱ 60 min
›
Materials
  • DC motor (high RPM)
  • AA battery pack
  • Cardboard chassis
  • Bottle cap wheels
  • Skewers + switch
Steps
  1. Build low flat cardboard chassis
  2. Mount rear axle with bottle cap wheels
  3. Connect motor directly to rear axle
  4. Wire battery with on/off switch
  5. Race against a friend's car!

Drive train mechanics — motor RPM, gear ratios, and wheel diameter all affect car speed. Kids discover gearing by trial and error.

PROJECT 92
Line Following Robot
Ages 12+Hard⏱ 120 min
›
Materials
  • 2 DC motors + wheels
  • 2 IR sensor modules (or LDR pairs)
  • 9V battery
  • Cardboard chassis
  • Black tape track on white paper
Steps
  1. Build 2-motor chassis with front castor wheel
  2. Mount two LDRs side by side at front
  3. Wire: left LDR over black → right motor slows
  4. Wire: right LDR over black → left motor slows
  5. Place on black tape track — it follows!
What You Learn

Differential drive and feedback control — the robot constantly corrects its path. Foundation of autonomous vehicle navigation!

Autonomous RobotsFeedback ControlAI Foundations

PROJECT 93
Robotic Hand
Ages 11+Hard⏱ 90 min
›
Materials
  • Cardboard hand template
  • 5 straws (finger channels)
  • String (tendons)
  • 5 individual pull controls
  • Tape + scissors
Steps
  1. Cut cardboard hand and finger segments
  2. Tape straw channels along each finger
  3. Thread string through straw channels
  4. Secure string at fingertip
  5. Pull individual strings to bend fingers — grip objects!
What You Learn

Tendon-driven robotics — mimics exactly how human fingers work. Foundation of prosthetic hands and industrial grippers.

BiomechanicsTendon DriveProsthetics

PROJECT 95
Electromagnetic Crane
Ages 11+Hard⏱ 75 min
›
Materials
  • Iron bolt (core)
  • Copper magnet wire
  • 9V battery + switch
  • Craft stick crane structure
  • Metal paper clips to lift
Steps
  1. Wrap 100+ turns of copper wire around bolt
  2. Connect wire ends to battery through switch
  3. Build craft stick crane arm
  4. Hang electromagnet from crane tip
  5. Switch on → picks up metal! Switch off → drops it!
What You Learn

Electromagnets — magnetism controlled by electricity. Same technology in scrap yard cranes, MRI machines, and electric motors!

ElectromagnetismSolenoidsIndustrial Tech

PROJECT 99
Pneumatic Arm
Ages 11+Hard⏱ 90 min
›
Materials
  • Balloons as air muscles
  • Plastic tubing
  • Cardboard arm structure
  • Hand pump or syringe
  • Tape + string
Steps
  1. Build 3-segment cardboard arm with pivot joints
  2. Attach inflatable balloon to each joint
  3. Connect balloons to tubes and pump
  4. Pump air → balloon inflates → arm bends!
  5. Control each joint independently for full movement
What You Learn

Pneumatic actuators — air pressure creates movement. Used in factory robots, airplane flaps, and soft robotics research!

PROJECT 100
Mini Weather Station
Ages 12+Hard⏱ 120 min
›
Materials
  • Thermistor (temperature)
  • LDR (light sensor)
  • Rain detector grid
  • Wind vane (compass + magnet)

Steps
  1. Wire thermistor to temperature LED scale (5 LEDs)

  5. Read weather data from your LED dashboard!

Multi-sensor data collection — your own IoT weather station! Combines everything learned in the guide: sensors, transistors, circuits, and displays.
